    Check your grouping on real feedback

    1. Take hand-coded answers to one survey question, across the range of lengths people write. Code the themes with a shared set of labels.
    2. Cut long answers to their first sentence, keeping those where the issue is still clear. Compare both versions against the same candidates and see whether the closest match changes theme.
    3. Run your usual grouping workflow on the hand-coded sample. How often do grouped pairs share a theme, and how often do pairs that share a theme end up together?
    4. Check both separately for two short answers, two long answers and mixed-length pairs. In these fictional comments, this was the check that showed the length problem. If the model struggles at some lengths, compare another embedding model the same way.
    How this demo was made

    Fengming Liu cut the same Reddit comments to different lengths. Average cosine rose with length for two of his three models, including BGE. A length-only placebo, which swapped each comment for a random one of similar length, reproduced 81% of the effect from his earlier study. To correct for length, he averaged cosine within word-count bands and weighted each band equally. This demo adapts his excerpt check and his placebo to nearest matches and to a grouping cut-off.

    The 48 fictional comments cover eight themes, six per theme, coded by hand. Each has a short version of 4 to 7 words and a long version that adds one sentence, for 26 to 31 words in total. Scores are cosine similarities between precomputed, normalized BGE embeddings (bge-base-en-v1.5).

    Step 1 uses each comment as the reference in turn and lengthens one of the other 47 at a time, which gives 48 × 47 = 2,256 single swaps. Step 2 attaches each of the other 46 extra sentences to the priorities comment. It leaves out the reference’s own sentence. Step 3 counts 1,128 pairs of two short comments, 1,128 pairs of two long comments and 2,256 short-and-long pairs, never pairing a comment with its own other version. About 11% of the pairs in each group share a theme.

    The default cut-off of 0.60 is where the two rates compared in step 3 meet. From 0.55 to 0.65, they stay within 8 points of each other. One person wrote all the long versions in a similar style, so some of the lift between two long comments may come from shared wording rather than length alone. Treat the numbers as an illustration, not as an estimate for real survey data.
